## 🚀 Current Master's Thesis Work

     **Abstract:**
     Burnt Mill Creek is an urban watershed located in the City of Wilmington, NC that traverses an underserved community and serves as a vital stormwater resource for ~23,700 people distributed over 4,200 acres. Recent hurricanes, heavy rainfall events, and king tides have led to significant flooding within the Burnt Mill Creek community. Urban areas such as Burnt Mill Creek are at higher risk due to more impervious surfaces (e.g., roadways, infrastructure) that increase runoff and flooding. Most existing flood literature, monitoring networks, models, and resilience plans focus on large spatial areas, which do not allow local governments to understand and appropriately prepare communities. To close this spatial and knowledge gap, the research proposed here involves the construction and deployment of real-time water level and precipitation sensors accompanied with water quality sensors that measure conductivity and temperature. I will analyze hydrodynamics using collected datasets from this area, incorporating data from both newly deployed and existing sensors. I will publicly publish all data on the Coastal Ocean Research and Monitoring Program website to inform communities effectively. I will also engage in community events and classroom visits in the Burnt Mill Creek community in order to increase literacy of coastal resiliency in this area.
